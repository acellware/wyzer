---
name: Medical Record Retention
type: industry-only
summary: >-
  How long medical and related compliance records must be kept. Health rules set
  retention periods for documentation and, for regulated electronic records, for
  the records themselves.
updated: 2026-09-04
cards:
  - framework: hipaa
    plain: >-
      HIPAA requires the documentation it mandates, such as policies, procedures,
      and certain records, to be kept for at least six years. Retention of the
      medical record itself is generally set by state law.
    detail: >-
      HIPAA Security and Privacy Rules documentation-retention requirement
      (45 CFR 164.316(b)(2) and 164.530(j)) sets a six-year minimum for required
      documentation.
    citations:
      - label: "HIPAA · 164.316(b) / 164.530(j)"
        url: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html
  - framework: fda
    plain: >-
      Where records are kept electronically under FDA rules, they must be retained
      for the period required for that record type and stay accurate and
      retrievable.
    detail: >-
      FDA 21 CFR Part 11.10(c) requires protection of records to enable their
      accurate and ready retrieval throughout the required retention period.
    citations:
      - label: "FDA · 21 CFR 11.10(c)"
        url: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11
---

**In practice.** Retention periods vary by record type and jurisdiction. Keep
records readable and retrievable for the full required period, then dispose of
them securely, and check the state or local rules that apply to you.
