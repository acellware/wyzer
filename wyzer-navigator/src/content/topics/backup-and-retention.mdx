---
name: Backups & Retention
type: resource-linked
summary: >-
  Whether data is backed up and kept for a defined period so it can be recovered
  after loss, corruption, or ransomware. Frameworks expect regular backups, a
  retention policy, and tested restores.
updated: 2026-09-04
cards:
  - framework: nist
    plain: >-
      Backups underpin recovery. They are expected to be created, protected, and
      tested so systems can be restored after an incident.
    detail: >-
      NIST CSF 2.0 Data Security (PR.DS) and Recovery (RC.RP) cover maintaining
      and executing recovery using reliable backups.
    source: NIST CSF 2.0 · PR.DS / RC.RP
    sourceUrl: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework
  - framework: soc2
    plain: >-
      Where you commit to availability, backup and recovery are expected controls
      so data and service can be restored.
    detail: >-
      Relates to the Availability criteria A1.2 and A1.3 (backup, recovery, and
      testing of recovery).
    source: SOC 2 TSC · A1.2 / A1.3
    sourceUrl: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2
  - framework: iso27001
    plain: >-
      Backup copies of information are expected to be taken and tested regularly,
      in line with a backup policy.
    detail: >-
      Annex A control A.8.13 (information backup) in ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
    source: ISO/IEC 27001:2022 · A.8.13
    sourceUrl: https://www.iso.org/standard/27001
  - framework: hipaa
    plain: >-
      A contingency plan is required, including a data backup plan and the ability
      to recover health information.
    detail: >-
      HIPAA Security Rule contingency-plan standard (45 CFR 164.308(a)(7)),
      including data backup and disaster recovery.
    source: HIPAA Security Rule · 164.308(a)(7)
    sourceUrl: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html
---

**In practice.** Automate backups, encrypt them, choose a retention window that
matches your recovery and regulatory needs, and periodically test that a restore
actually works.
