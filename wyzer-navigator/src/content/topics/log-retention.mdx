---
name: Log Retention
type: resource-linked
summary: >-
  How long logs are kept. Frameworks expect logs to be retained long enough to
  support investigations, with PCI DSS setting a specific minimum period.
updated: 2026-09-04
cards:
  - framework: nist
    plain: >-
      Logs are expected to be retained long enough to support detecting,
      analyzing, and responding to incidents.
    detail: >-
      NIST CSF 2.0 Detection (DE.CM) and Analysis (RS.AN) rely on retained log
      history to investigate events.
    source: NIST CSF 2.0 · DE.CM / RS.AN
    sourceUrl: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework
  - framework: soc2
    plain: >-
      Enough log history is expected to be retained to support monitoring and to
      investigate security incidents.
    detail: >-
      Supports CC7.2 and CC7.3 (evaluating and responding to security events).
    source: SOC 2 TSC · CC7.2 / CC7.3
    sourceUrl: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2
  - framework: iso27001
    plain: >-
      Logs are expected to be kept for an appropriate period and protected from
      tampering and unauthorized access.
    detail: >-
      Annex A control A.8.15 (logging) in ISO/IEC 27001:2022 covers retention and
      protection of logs.
    source: ISO/IEC 27001:2022 · A.8.15
    sourceUrl: https://www.iso.org/standard/27001
  - framework: pci-dss
    plain: >-
      Audit log history must be kept for at least 12 months, with the most recent
      3 months readily available for analysis.
    detail: >-
      PCI DSS v4.0 Requirement 10.5.1 sets the 12-month retention and 3-month
      immediate-availability expectation.
    source: PCI DSS v4.0 · Requirement 10.5.1
    sourceUrl: https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/
---

**In practice.** Keep at least a year of logs where regulated data is involved,
store them immutably so they cannot be altered, and balance retention against
privacy obligations for any personal data in the logs.
