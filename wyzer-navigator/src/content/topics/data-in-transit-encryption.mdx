---
name: Data in Transit (TLS)
type: resource-linked
summary: >-
  Whether data is encrypted while moving across a network, between clients,
  services, and databases. Frameworks broadly expect sensitive data to travel
  over encrypted channels such as TLS rather than in the clear.
updated: 2026-09-04
cards:
  - framework: nist
    plain: >-
      Expects data in transit to be protected, typically by encrypting network
      communications that carry sensitive information.
    detail: >-
      NIST CSF 2.0 Data Security (PR.DS) includes protecting the confidentiality
      and integrity of data-in-transit, generally through transport encryption.
    citations:
      - label: "NIST CSF 2.0 · PR.DS"
        url: https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework
  - framework: soc2
    plain: >-
      Encrypted transport is a standard control for protecting data as it moves
      between users and systems.
    detail: >-
      Maps to CC6.7 (restricting the transmission of information and using
      encryption to protect it in transit).
    citations:
      - label: "SOC 2 TSC · CC6.7"
        url: https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2
  - framework: iso27001
    plain: >-
      Expects information in transit, including across public networks, to be
      protected, in practice through transport-layer encryption.
    detail: >-
      Relevant Annex A controls in ISO/IEC 27001:2022 include A.8.24 (use of
      cryptography) and A.8.20 (networks security).
    citations:
      - label: "ISO/IEC 27001:2022 · A.8.20 / A.8.24"
        url: https://www.iso.org/standard/27001
  - framework: gdpr
    plain: >-
      Where personal data is transmitted, encryption in transit is a common
      measure for keeping the transfer secure and appropriate to the risk.
    detail: >-
      Article 32 requires appropriate technical measures for security of
      processing; encrypted transport is a widely used measure for data in
      transit.
    citations:
      - label: "GDPR · Article 32"
        url: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj
  - framework: hipaa
    plain: >-
      Treats encryption of health information sent over networks as an addressable
      safeguard, expected wherever transmission could expose the data.
    detail: >-
      The HIPAA Security Rule transmission-security standard includes encryption
      as an addressable specification (45 CFR 164.312(e)(2)(ii)).
    citations:
      - label: "HIPAA Security Rule · 164.312(e)"
        url: https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/index.html
  - framework: pci-dss
    plain: >-
      Requires strong cryptography whenever cardholder data crosses open or public
      networks.
    detail: >-
      PCI DSS v4.0 Requirement 4 covers protecting cardholder data with strong
      cryptography during transmission over open, public networks.
    citations:
      - label: "PCI DSS v4.0 · Requirement 4"
        url: https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/
---

**In practice.** This usually means enforcing TLS (HTTPS) on every endpoint,
requiring encrypted connections to databases and caches, and redirecting or
rejecting plain-text traffic rather than allowing it as a fallback.
